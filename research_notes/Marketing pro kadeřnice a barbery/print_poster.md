Method note: These notes come from web search result summaries (Nov 2026). Full-page fetches of several primary sources (nielseniq.com, newsroom.gettyimages.com, frontiersin.org, cxnetwork.com, mycreativeshop.com) failed because those hosts are blocked by this session's network settings. Figures below are therefore taken from search-result extracts of those pages, not from a full read. Most salon and QR material is vendor content (print shops, booking software, QR code generators) with a commercial interest, and is flagged as such.

Poster under critique: a light grey AI-generated photo of a black-gloved hand holding a clipper; a huge serif "ŠŤASTNÁ"; "Kateřina" in italic; small caps "Pánské střihy"; an orange round sticker "NOVĚ od 1. 12."; "od 179 Kč" in orange; an info block (address, phone, Instagram, "termín na domluvě"). It has no face, no QR code, no offer with a deadline and no examples of her work.

## 1. What makes a salon/barber poster or flyer convert (hierarchy, 3-second rule, single CTA, offer with deadline, before/after, social proof)

### Takeaway
Practitioner guidance agrees on a few points: one dominant headline that states a benefit or hook, a reading order of hook, then who or what, then action and details, exactly one primary call to action, and a concrete offer with an expiry date. The "3-second rule" is a heuristic, not a measured standard. Academic work supports the claim that deadlines speed up action. In barber marketing, before/after photos of real work are the main trust tool.

### Cited Findings
- Passersby decide within about 3 seconds whether a flyer is relevant. Readers "do not study them—they scan." The source is print-vendor and design-blog consensus, and the 3-second figure is a rule of thumb with no study behind it. — [Overnight Prints: What people read first on flyers](https://blog.overnightprints.com/what-people-read-first-on-flyers/); [4over4: How to design flyers](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/how-to-design-flyers)
- Recommended reading order: the big benefit or hook, then who or what it's about, then the action and details (date, time, price, phone, URL). The headline is "the single most important piece of text on the flyer." — [Overnight Prints](https://blog.overnightprints.com/what-people-read-first-on-flyers/); [Made Good Designs: Flyer design principles](https://madegooddesigns.com/flyer-design-principles/)
- "Every flyer needs exactly one primary call to action," and it should be "bold, clear, and impossible to miss." — [4over4 flyer guide](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/how-to-design-flyers); [Venngage: How to design a flyer](https://venngage.com/blog/how-to-design-a-flyer/)
- Size is "the bluntest and most reliable hierarchy tool." The other levers are contrast, typography, spacing and colour. Test: shrink the design to about 2 inches tall, and if the headline and CTA still register, the hierarchy works. — [4over4: Guide to hierarchy in graphic design](https://mapp.4over4.com/guide/guide-to-hierarchy-in-graphic-design)
- For salon posters, "a cluttered poster can confuse and overwhelm potential clients." Recommendation: a simple message with a clear CTA. — [MyCreativeShop: Hair salon poster tips](https://mycreativeshop.com/learn/7-eye-catching-hair-salon-poster-tips-to-make-your-business-shine) (vendor; seen via search extract only)
- New-shop flyers should include "special promotions, giveaways, or events to entice customers." Track flyer results with unique promo codes or by asking new clients how they heard about you. — [MyCreativeShop: 10 tips for barber shop flyers](https://www.mycreativeshop.com/learn/10-tips-for-barber-shop-flyers-that-catch-everyones-eye) (vendor; search extract)
- Intro offers: Mindbody advises "a clear expiration date," because booking a new client sooner and seeing them more often improves retention. — [Mindbody: 3 salon intro offers](https://www.mindbodyonline.com/business/education/blog/3-salon-intro-offers-help-retain-new-clients)
- An expiry date in an offer's terms "adds a sense of urgency." The terms should also state eligibility conditions. — [Appointy blog](https://blog.appointy.com/?p=22625)
- Fresha says a clear end date encourages clients to act and refer others. — [Fresha: attract new and existing clients](https://www.fresha.com/blog/attract-both-new-and-existing-clients)
- Booksy suggests a clear new-client special, e.g. a referral where the new client gets 25% off the first service. A sample flyer line: "Bring this flyer to your first appointment for 25% off." — [Booksy: Salon promotion ideas that work](https://biz.booksy.com/en-gb/blog/salon-promotions-that-work-proven-ideas-to-attract-more-clients)
- Academic evidence on deadlines: Aggarwal & Vaidyanathan (2003, older) found that time-limiting a promotion sped up purchases and reduced search for better deals. The finding is reported secondhand in Noone & Lin (J. Hospitality & Tourism Research). — [NCCU-hosted paper PDF](https://ah.lib.nccu.edu.tw/bitstream/140.119/111932/1/2015-62.pdf); [Psychology Today: Why limited-time offers entice shoppers](https://psychologytoday.com/za/blog/the-science-behind-behavior/201906/why-limited-time-offers-entice-shoppers-buy) (2019)
- Hmurovic & Lamberton (2022, Journal of Marketing Research): time-scarcity promotions worked better when the timeframe's cause was outside the retailer's control, such as a holiday or the customer's birthday. This finding is from a secondary summary. — [Exel Magazine summary](https://exelmagazine.org/?p=8019)
- Deadline length is a trade-off. Longer windows raise awareness of an offer, but they reduce urgency and invite indefinite delay. Redemptions tend to peak right after an offer is issued, with a surge just before expiry. — [Optimizing time limits in retail promotions (Bohrium abstract)](https://www.bohrium.com/paper-details/optimizing-time-limits-in-retail-promotions-an-email-application/812330840221548544-6912); [Psychology Today](https://psychologytoday.com/za/blog/the-science-behind-behavior/201906/why-limited-time-offers-entice-shoppers-buy)
- Before/after photos: "Most people have trust issues when it comes to their hair. To win a potential client's trust, there's nothing better than good ole before and after photos." — [Swipefile: Barber Instagram before and after](https://swipefile.com/barber-instagram-before-and-after)
- Before-and-after content showcases craft and "build[s] authority and trust before someone sits in the chair." It also recommends featuring the team's personalities. — [GlossGenius: Barber marketing tips](https://glossgenius.com/blog/barber-marketing-tips-to-grow-barbershop); [Brandignity: Barber shop marketing strategy (2024)](https://brandignity.com/2024/10/barber-shop-marketing-strategy/)
- Use consistent angles, lighting and background for transformation photos. — [Swipefile](https://swipefile.com/barber-instagram-before-and-after); [GlossGenius](https://glossgenius.com/blog/barber-marketing-tips-to-grow-barbershop)
- Real photos of actual work are "the most authentic and effective marketing content for salons." This comes from an AI-image vendor, which makes the admission notable. — [zsky.ai: AI for hair salons](https://zsky.ai/blog/ai-for-hair-salons)

### Inferences
- Applied to the Šťastná poster: the dominant element is the surname, which is neither a benefit nor a hook for a passerby who doesn't know her. "Pánské střihy" (what the service is) sits in small caps, so the hierarchy is inverted against the recommended order of hook, then who or what, then action.
- The poster has no single CTA. "termín na domluvě" is passive and lists no channel, while phone, Instagram and address compete equally. A single CTA such as "Rezervuj přes Instagram / zavolej 7xx…" or a QR code labelled with its payoff would fix this.
- "NOVĚ od 1. 12." is an opening announcement, not an offer, because it has no deadline and no reason to act now. "od 179 Kč" is a price floor, not a promotion. An intro offer with an end date tied to an external event (e.g. "první střih 149 Kč do 31. 12.") aligns with the Mindbody, Appointy and Hmurovic & Lamberton findings. A Christmas or New Year deadline counts as an "external cause."
- With zero work examples, she has no trust proof as a new barber, and per practitioner sources that is the main barrier to a first booking.

### Gaps
- I found no controlled study measuring conversion of salon/barber posters specifically. All salon-specific advice is practitioner or vendor opinion.
- I found no published response-rate benchmarks for in-salon A3 posters (vs. door-drop flyers).
- I found no source on social proof (reviews, star ratings) on printed salon posters specifically.

## 2. QR codes on print ads (scan rates, best practices, placement, telling people what they get)

### Takeaway
QR scanning is mainstream: per Uniqode's 2025 survey, only 8% of US consumers have never scanned one. Czech consumers are familiar with QR codes through QR payments, though by ČNB/Ipsos data only about half have paid by QR. Vendor sources consistently say the CTA text next to the code ("Scan to…" plus the payoff) and its placement drive scan rate more than anything else. Independent print-specific scan benchmarks don't exist.

### Cited Findings
- In Uniqode's State of QR Codes 2026 (consumer survey run October 2025), 44% of consumers scan weekly or daily, and only 8% have never scanned a QR code. About 70% of US consumers scan at least monthly. — [Uniqode: State of QR Codes 2026](https://www.uniqode.com/unique-angles/playbooks/state-of-qr-codes-2026); [Uniqode QR statistics](https://www.uniqode.com/blog/qr-code-statistics) (vendor)
- The same analysis of 188 million scans in 2025 ranks top scan contexts: restaurants 58%, websites 42%, product packaging 40%. — [Uniqode State of QR Codes 2026](https://www.uniqode.com/unique-angles/playbooks/state-of-qr-codes-2026) (vendor)
- 58% of consumers feel somewhat or very confident QR codes are safe to scan, and 14% have encountered a QR scam. — [Uniqode QR statistics](https://www.uniqode.com/blog/qr-code-statistics) (vendor)
- Uniqode reports an average scan-through rate of 12.8% across industries. This is cross-channel and not specific to print. — [Uniqode blog](https://www.uniqode.com/blog/?p=8124) (vendor)
- Bitly: 40% of marketers using QR codes place them in print ads. This measures adoption, not performance. — [Bitly: Pros and cons of QR code advertising 2026](https://bitly.com/blog/pros-and-cons-of-ads-with-qr-codes/)
- Placement and CTA copy have the largest measurable effect on scan rates, "typically 30–80% lift in controlled tests." The methodology is undocumented, so treat this with caution. — [Linkbreakers: QR A/B testing benchmarks](https://linkbreakers.com/help/article/qr-code-ab-testing-benchmarks-variables-that-affect-scan-rates)
- Best practice is to state the payoff ("Scan to save 20%"). Vague messaging is a common cause of low engagement. — [QR Code Tiger: QR scan rate tactics](https://www.qrcode-tiger.com/qr-code-scan-rate); [Oppizi: Improving QR scan rate](https://oppizi.com/ca/en/?p=14005); [GS1 UK: "Give consumers a reason to scan"](https://www.gs1uk.org/knowledge-hub/qr-codes-powered-by-gs1/five-ways-to-increase-consumer-adoption-of-qr-codes-powered)
- Minimum reliable size is about 2.5 cm (1 inch) square at hand distance. Use a dark code on a light background. — [QR Code Tiger](https://www.qrcode-tiger.com/qr-code-scan-rate); [Oppizi](https://oppizi.com/ca/en/?p=14005)
- Use one QR code (dynamic, trackable) per placement for attribution, and track post-scan conversions, not just scans. — [QR Codes Unlimited: Track offline ad performance](https://qrcodesunlimited.com/academy/how-to-use-qr-codes-to-track-offline-ad-performance)
- Czech context: in an Ipsos survey for ČNB, 47% of Czechs said they had never paid by QR code, so roughly half have. Only one in five small businesses offers QR payment at the point of sale. — [ČNB press release (EN)](https://www.cnb.cz/en/cnb-news/press-releases/CNB-instant-payments-save-businesses-time-and-money-yet-only-one-in-five-currently-use-QR-codes-at-the-point-of-sale); [Expats.cz](https://www.expats.cz/czech-news/article/more-than-half-of-czechs-now-use-instant-payments-do-you-pay-by-qr-code)

### Inferences
- For a poster in a salon or window, a QR code with an explicit payoff label (e.g. "Naskenuj → volné termíny / rezervace" or "→ ukázky střihů na IG") turns the passive "termín na domluvě" into an action someone can take while waiting or walking by. Czech audiences recognise QR codes from payments.
- For a window poster read from a few metres, the code must be larger than the 2.5 cm minimum. The usual rule of thumb is a distance-to-size ratio of about 10:1, but that rule isn't sourced here (see Gaps). A code of roughly 4–6 cm would suit a passerby at 0.5–1 m.
- A dynamic code would let her measure whether the poster works, as the attribution advice above recommends.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, print-specific QR scan-rate benchmark. All figures come from vendors.
- I found no Czech-specific statistic on scanning marketing QR codes, as opposed to payment QR codes.
- I didn't verify a sourced distance-to-size ratio for QR codes in this session.

## 3. Human faces / eye contact vs. product/tool imagery; authenticity vs. stock/AI imagery (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
Eye-tracking research shows that faces in ads raise attention, memory and purchase intent. A face gazing toward the offer steers attention to it better than direct gaze. Consumer research from 2024–2026 consistently finds that AI-generated imagery lowers trust and memorability, and AI images of people or real products are judged more misleading than AI images of other subjects. The penalty is largest when authenticity matters, as with personal services.

### Cited Findings
- Adil, Lacoste-Badie & Droulers (2018, Journal of Advertising Research; older), an eye-tracking study of print ads: face presence and gaze directed toward the product, versus no face and gaze toward the viewer, have "a strong influence on attention to and memorization of advertisements, on advertisement and brand evaluation, and on purchase intention." — [HAL: Face Presence and Gaze Direction in Print Advertisements](https://hal.archives-ouvertes.fr/halshs-01910205)
- Palcu, Sudkamp & Florack (2017, Frontiers in Psychology; older): in banner ads, a face gazing toward the product increased the likelihood of viewers looking at the product, and it increased later purchase intention. — [PMC5454066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5454066/)
- Droulers & Adil (2015; older): magazine ads with gaze toward the product improved memory for brand and message information compared with direct gaze. This is reported via the HAL and PMC records above. — [PMC5454066](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5454066/)
- NielsenIQ (Dec 12, 2024; >2,000 participants, EEG on ~150): AI-generated ads, "even those rated high quality," produced weaker memory activation. Consumers intuitively identified most AI ads and found them "annoying," "boring," and "confusing," with a possible "negative halo" on the brand. Low-quality AI visuals raise cognitive effort and distract from the message. — [NIQ press release](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2024/niq-research-uncovers-hidden-consumer-attitudes-toward-ai-generated-ads/); [Business Wire copy](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20241212354811/en/NIQ-Research-Uncovers-Hidden-Consumer-Attitudes-Toward-AI-Generated-Ads) (not peer-reviewed)
- Getty Images VisualGPS "Building Trust in the Age of AI" (April 2024; four studies July 2022–Sept 2023, >30,000 adults): almost 90% of consumers want to know whether an image is AI-made, and 98% say authentic images and video are pivotal to trust. 78% say an AI-generated image cannot be considered "authentic." Consumers are more than twice as likely to prefer real images (38%) over AI images (15%) in marketing. AI depictions of non-human subjects were seen as less misleading than AI images of people or real products. — [Getty Images newsroom](https://newsroom.gettyimages.com/en/getty-images/nearly-90-of-consumers-want-transparency-on-ai-images-finds-getty-images-report); [VisualGPS report PDF](https://reports.gettyimages.com/VisualGPS-Building-Trust-In-AI.pdf); [Getty VisualGPS page](https://www.gettyimages.com/visualgps/creative-trends/technology/building-trust-in-the-age-of-ai) (stock-photo vendor with a commercial interest)
- Getty's guidance is that AI content "is not the right fit for campaigns centered on authenticity or real people." — [Getty VisualGPS](https://www.gettyimages.com/visualgps/creative-trends/technology/building-trust-in-the-age-of-ai)
- Survey summary (via CX Network): about 7 in 10 consumers trust ads made entirely by humans, 4 in 10 when AI tools are used, and about 2 in 10 when an ad is generated by AI alone. In a Cint survey of 1,000 US consumers, 46% dislike AI-generated people in ads and 74% say brands should disclose AI use. — [CX Network: Are AI-generated images putting customers off?](https://www.cxnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/interviews/ai-generated-images-customer-trust-advertising) (secondary; underlying survey dates unconfirmed)
- Counterpoint: a Publicis Media / Yahoo study of 1,200 US consumers found that disclosing AI use raised ad appeal (+47%), trustworthiness (+73%) and brand trust (+96%) among those who noticed the disclosure. Consumers in SE Asia, LatAm, the UAE and Saudi Arabia are "much more receptive" to AI imagery (Getty). — [CX Network](https://www.cxnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/interviews/ai-generated-images-customer-trust-advertising); [Adweek: People want to know when AI is used in ads](https://www.adweek.com/media/people-want-to-know-when-ai-is-used-in-ads-research-confirms/)
- Attitude and behaviour diverge: in Attest's test, only about a quarter of respondents correctly identified an AI-generated image. — [CX Network](https://www.cxnetwork.com/artificial-intelligence/interviews/ai-generated-images-customer-trust-advertising); [Imgix: When AI visual expectations clash with reality](https://www.imgix.com/blog/ai-visual-expectations)
- 2026 framing: "When everything looks like AI, being real becomes the advantage." — [PYMNTS (2026)](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2026/when-everything-looks-like-ai-being-real-becomes-the-advantage/); [Harris Poll UK: The authenticity trade-off](https://www.theharrispoll.co.uk/insights/blog/the-authenticity-trade-off-how-ai-is-reshaping-trust-in-advertisingnbsp)

### Inferences
- The poster's hero image is an AI-generated hand with a tool. It has no face, no gaze cue and no real work, so it gives up the attention and memory benefits of faces. It also falls in the category Getty and NIQ flag as the worst fit for AI: a "real product/person" for a personal, trust-based service. A generic black-gloved clipper hand reads as stock and doesn't show who will cut your hair.
- Being a woman barber is a differentiator in a male-dominated trade, and the poster hides it, since the only cue is the feminine surname ending. A real photo of Kateřina, ideally looking toward the headline or offer (per the gaze-cue studies) or making confident eye contact, would make the differentiator visible and humanise the new chair. This is inference; no study tested female barbers specifically.
- Replacing or supplementing the image with 2–3 real cuts by her (before/after or finished fades) addresses both trust and authenticity.

### Gaps
- I found no study specifically on faces vs. tools in hair or barber advertising.
- I found no Czech or Central European data on trust in AI imagery. Getty notes regional variation but gave no CZ figures in the extracts.
- The direct-gaze vs. averted-gaze findings come from product ads. For a personal-service poster, where the person is the product, direct eye contact may work differently, and I found no source on this.

## 4. In-salon placement (eye level, mirror stations, checkout), readability distance, paper

### Takeaway
Salon-industry guidance favours three spots: reception/entry at eye level without blocking traffic, the styling station/mirror (where attention is held for a long time), and the window. Sign-industry rules of thumb give about 25 mm of letter height per ~10 m for glance legibility, and roughly 2–3 mm cap height per metre for body text. All placement advice is practitioner opinion; I found no controlled studies.

### Cited Findings
- Place displays near the reception desk, visible on entry, at eye level, without obstructing traffic. Reception suits "service bundles of the month" and social media plaques or hashtag flyers. — [The Salon Business: Retail display optimization guide](https://thesalonbusiness.com/wp-content/uploads/Salon-Retail-Display-Optimization-Guide-68.pdf); [Zenoti: Salon signage ideas](https://www.zenoti.com/thecheckin/9-salon-signage-ideas-to-attract-more-guests-and-increase-revenue)
- Redken: a client's attention is held for about 45 minutes at a workstation. Keep the station uncluttered and use it as a conversation starter. — [Redken Pro: How to merchandise](https://www.redkenpro.com/business/salon-success-kit/redken-business-tools/how-to-merchandise)
- Mirror items should sit at the mirror corner and not block the stylist's view. One salon placed mirror hashtag signs low, to be "subtle not intrusive." — [Crown TV: Digital signage for salons](https://www.crowntv-us.com/blog/digital-signage-for-salons-and-spas-to-upgrade); [Salon Today: Cheeky Strut selfie station](https://www.salontoday.com/articles/cheeky-struts-selfie-station-ignites-salon-marketing)
- A Salon Today STAMP honoree reported a 350% increase in an add-on service after adding it to mirror clings. This is a single anecdote. — [Salon Today: 2022 STAMP honorees for in-salon marketing](https://www.salontoday.com/articles/2022-stamp-honorees-for-in-salon-marketing) (2022)
- Storefront and window signage is "advertising you already paid for" and can attract walk-ins. — [4over4: Salon signage ideas to attract walk-ins](https://www.4over4.com/article/10-salon-signage-ideas-to-attract-walk-ins)
- Legibility rule of thumb: about 1 inch (25 mm) of letter height per 10 feet (about 3 m) for best impact, with a maximum readable distance about 3× greater. One metric poster guide gives 2–3 mm cap height per metre for body text, with headlines 3–4× that. These are heuristics, not standards. — [Signs.com: Letter height visibility](https://www.signs.com/blog/?p=16381); [Signazon chart](https://signazon.com/help-center/sign-letter-height-visibility-chart.aspx); [4over4: Poster typography readable from 30 feet](https://v2.4over4.com/guide/poster-typography-design-tips-for-eye-catching-prints); [Studio Matrx: Legibility, size and viewing distance](https://www.studiomatrx.org/students/wayfinding-and-environmental-graphics/legibility-size-and-viewing-distance)
- Distinction: legibility ("can you make out the word at a glance") vs. readability ("comfortably read the passage"). Height rules cover only legibility. — [Studio Matrx](https://www.studiomatrx.org/students/wayfinding-and-environmental-graphics/legibility-size-and-viewing-distance)

### Inferences
- An A3 sheet is 297 × 420 mm. Using 2–3 mm cap height per metre, info-block text read from 2 m needs about 5–6 mm caps (roughly 20–24 pt+), and a window read from 4–5 m needs about 10–15 mm. The poster's small-caps "Pánské střihy" and info block are likely below that for window viewing. The surname is large enough but carries the least useful information.
- Light grey photo plus orange text is a low-contrast risk at distance. The hierarchy-contrast guidance above implies the price and sticker should be checked for contrast; behind glass, reflections make this worse.
- Two versions would fit two contexts. A window version needs the service, face, offer and QR legible at 2–4 m. A mirror/reception version can carry more detail plus business cards.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced guidance on paper stock (matte vs. gloss, weight) for salon posters. Matte is commonly recommended for glare reduction behind glass, but I didn't verify a source.
- I found no measured data on which in-salon location drives the most bookings.
- I found no guidance specific to chair renters advertising inside a host salon (etiquette, competing with the host's branding).

## 5. Common mistakes in barber/salon flyers cited by marketers

### Takeaway
Marketers repeatedly cite clutter, vague or missing calls to action, no reason to act now (no offer or deadline), no tracking mechanism, generic visuals instead of real work, and designs that serve aesthetics over the message. Evidence quality is low (vendor blogs), but the points are consistent across sources.

### Cited Findings
- Clutter confuses and overwhelms, so keep one clear CTA. — [MyCreativeShop: Hair salon poster tips](https://mycreativeshop.com/learn/7-eye-catching-hair-salon-poster-tips-to-make-your-business-shine)
- "Each design choice should support the message." Common design errors make flyers look unprofessional. — [PromoCorner: Avoid these 5 common flyer mistakes](https://promocorner.com/promojournal/Avoid-These-5-Common-Flyer-Mistakes/?i=6899)
- Vague or generic messaging is a common cause of low engagement. Give a reason to respond, e.g. "Scan to save 20%." — [Oppizi](https://oppizi.com/ca/en/?p=14005); [QR Code Tiger](https://www.qrcode-tiger.com/qr-code-scan-rate)
- Without a tracking device (promo code, "how did you hear about us," unique QR), you can't tell whether flyers work. — [MyCreativeShop barber flyer tips](https://www.mycreativeshop.com/learn/10-tips-for-barber-shop-flyers-that-catch-everyones-eye); [QR Codes Unlimited](https://qrcodesunlimited.com/academy/how-to-use-qr-codes-to-track-offline-ad-performance)
- Opening flyers without a promotion or event miss the hook needed to "entice customers to check out your new shop." — [MyCreativeShop barber flyer tips](https://www.mycreativeshop.com/learn/10-tips-for-barber-shop-flyers-that-catch-everyones-eye)
- Offers without clear terms (expiry, eligibility) cause problems. One source warns that a birthday discount without proof requirements "can be claimed by anyone." — [Booksy promotion ideas](https://biz.booksy.com/en-gb/blog/salon-promotions-that-work-proven-ideas-to-attract-more-clients); [Appointy](https://blog.appointy.com/?p=22625)
- Recommended against: synthetic content instead of real work, since real photos of actual work are the most authentic content. — [zsky.ai](https://zsky.ai/blog/ai-for-hair-salons)
- New owners underestimate that marketing is "business skills," not barber skills, and it needs deliberate effort. — [Vinci: 7 most expensive mistakes new barbershop owners make](https://vinci-26-website.pages.dev/blog/expensive-mistakes-new-barbershop-owners)

### Inferences
Mapped against the poster:
1. The name is the headline instead of a benefit or hook.
2. There's no single CTA, and "termín na domluvě" is passive.
3. There's no offer with a deadline. "NOVĚ od 1. 12." is an announcement, and "od 179 Kč" is a price floor.
4. The image is AI-generated and generic, with no face and no real work.
5. There's no QR code or tracking.
6. Key info is in small caps, light-on-light and orange-on-grey, which is a contrast and legibility risk.
7. The differentiator (a woman barber, new in this salon) isn't communicated.
- Strengths to keep: a clear, premium-looking typographic identity; a visible price anchor (useful for a price-sensitive men's-cut market); and a complete info block.

### Gaps
- The "5 Biggest Mistakes Barbers Make in Their Marketing" article (Issuu, Barbershop magazine) was found, but its list wasn't readable in the extract. — [Issuu](https://issuu.com/mochagroup/docs/barbershop_yr11iss3/s/16844342)
- I found no Czech-language sources on salon poster mistakes in this session.
- Booksy, Phorest, Vagaro, Square and Modern Salon pages specifically on print or poster design weren't surfaced. Their guidance found here concerns offers, not print layout.
